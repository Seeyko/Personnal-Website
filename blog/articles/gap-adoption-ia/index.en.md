Twitter/X, my tech friends, my colleagues at CBA. We all talk about AI. Claude Code here, agents there. It seems omnipresent.

**But we're in a bubble.**

Step out of Twitter. Step out of tech Discord servers. Talk to traditional French companies. Talk to devs who aren't in the startup ecosystem.

**10% of the world really uses AI. Even fewer use agentic systems.**

The gap isn't future. It's already here. Now. March 2026.

## The Observed Gap

These past weeks, I've talked with devs, companies, CTOs. Outside my bubble. Here's what I found.

### Most Haven't Switched

Not because they're incompetent. Not because they don't know AI exists. But because there's real friction.

**The obstacles I see**:

**Lack of time**: teams already overloaded. Impossible to take 2 weeks to "test AI". Need immediate results, not experimentation.

**Uncertainty**: "we use ChatGPT for snippets, then what?" Not clear how to transform entire workflows. Fear of making the wrong choice.

**Hype/reality gap**: articles talking about "10x productivity". Reality more nuanced. Difficulty distinguishing bullshit from real.

**Fear of wrong path**: investing time in a tool that will be obsolete in 6 months. Which tool to choose? Cursor? Claude Code? GitHub Copilot? All?

### What I Feel

A gap widening between those who switched and the others. Not in 5 years. Now. Those who master have a clear advantage.

### The Numbers That Confirm

**9.7% of US companies use AI** (Anthropic Economic Index, August 2025). So 90%+ don't use it yet.

**60%+ of EU companies at the earliest stage** (WEF, 2025). France and Germany particularly risk-averse.

**88% of organizations say they use AI, but only 1% consider themselves "mature"** (McKinsey, 2025). The gap between declaration and reality is huge.

### Productivity: The Debate

Studies contradict each other. Some say AI makes devs **19% slower** (METR, July 2025). Others talk about **+10% velocity** (Google, Q1 2025).

**My observation**: no universal "10x magic". But for those who master: real, measurable gains. The key: rethinking workflows, not just "using a tool".

## My Last 3 Months — The Most Satisfying Game

I wrote [One Year After Hello World](https://tomandrieu.com/blog/un-an-apres-hello-world). Here's what happened these past 3 months.

### Learning and Grinding XP Much Faster

**Before (without LLM)**: learning a new stack took 2-3 weeks. Read docs, test, break, restart. Lots of downtime.

**Now (with LLM)**: learning a new stack takes 2-3 days. Claude guides me, explains, corrects in real-time. No downtime: I learn while producing.

### Productive Dev While Learning

The paradox: before, either I learned or I produced. Now, I learn **while** producing. Claude explains while I code. I understand by building.

### An Incredibly Satisfying Game

Why "game"?

**Ultra-fast feedback loops**: I test an idea, Claude corrects me immediately. Iteration in seconds, not hours.

**XP visibly climbing fast**: every day I learn something new. Not just "how to code X", but "why architect like this", "how to think about this problem", "what's the right abstraction".

**Constant progression feeling**: new skills unlocked regularly. It's addictive.

What it teaches me: not just code. But architecture. Systems thinking. Product thinking. Claude doesn't just code for me — he explains why.

### The Top 10% Agentic

I certainly consider myself in the **top 10% of devs with agentic systems**. Not by innate talent. But because I've grinded intensively these past months.

BMAD. Claude Code terminal-first. Spec-driven, test-driven. Olympus/Pantheon (useful failure). MVP BVN, scanr, personal site. CBA hackathon this week: 1 week to recode a big chunk of the app.

**But this advantage won't last.**

### The Gap Will Close in 18-24 Months

Why?

**LLMs are becoming easier to use.** Anthropic, OpenAI, Google — all working to simplify UX. What took me 2 weeks to master will take 2 days in 1 year.

**Tools are improving.** Claude Code, Cursor, all improving exponentially. The learning curve is shrinking.

**Adoption is accelerating.** 84% of devs use or plan to use AI (vs 76% in 2025). More people switch every month.

What this means: my current advantage is temporary. Today's top 10% will be tomorrow's top 30%.

**By end of 2027, tools will be trivial.** Everyone will have access to the same power. The advantage will be pure talent again, not tool mastery.

**I must seize this gap now.**

## Seizing the Gap to Share My Vision

I have 18-24 months. Maybe less. To leverage this advantage before it dilutes.

### What I Want to Do

**Not just "code faster".** Anyone will do that in 2 years.

What matters: sharing the vision. Helping other teams make this transition.

**Training + support**:

- Audit current workflows
- Training BMAD + Claude Code
- Support pilot projects
- Mandatory knowledge transfer (not just deliver and leave)

**Not selling AI**: not "buy this magic SaaS". Transform how you work. Structure human-AI collaboration.

**Not just for devs**: marketing, ops, support. Everywhere there are structurable workflows.

### For Whom

SMEs and tech startups. Dev teams. Companies that feel the gap but don't know where to start.

**Why now**: because in 18-24 months, this expertise will be commoditized. Tools will be trivial. Everyone will have access.

Today, I have an advantage. I can help others shorten their learning curve.

Tomorrow, everyone will be at the same level.

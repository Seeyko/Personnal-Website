Twitter/X, my tech friends, my colleagues at CBA. We all talk about AI. Claude Code here, agents there. It seems omnipresent. But we're in a bubble. Get out of Twitter. Get out of tech Discord servers. Talk to classic French companies. Talk to devs who aren't in the startup ecosystem. 10% of the world actually uses AI. Even fewer use agentics. The gap isn't future. It's already here. Now. March 2026.

## The gap I've observed

These past few weeks, I've talked with devs, companies, CTOs. Outside my bubble. And I noticed something. Most haven't switched. Not because they're incompetent. Not because they don't know AI exists. But because there's real friction.

The first obstacle is lack of time. Teams are already overloaded. Impossible to take 2 weeks to "test AI". Need immediate results, not experimentation. Then there's uncertainty. "We use ChatGPT for snippets, and then what?" Not clear how to transform entire workflows. Fear of making the wrong choice. The gap between hype and reality complicates everything. Articles talking about "10x productivity". Reality more nuanced. Hard to distinguish bullshit from real. And then there's the fear of investing time in a tool that will be obsolete in 6 months. Which tool to choose? Cursor? Claude Code? GitHub Copilot? All of them? None?

What I feel is a growing gap between those who switched and the others. Not in 5 years. Now. Those who master it have a clear advantage. The numbers confirm what I observe. According to the [Anthropic Economic Index from September 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-economic-index-september-2025-report), only 9.7% of American companies use AI. So 90%+ don't use it yet. In Europe, it's even worse. The [WEF reports in 2025](https://www.weforum.org/stories/2025/09/europe-ai-adoption-lag/) that over 60% of European companies are at the earliest stage of maturity. France and Germany particularly risk-averse.

There's a huge gap between declaration and reality. [McKinsey shows in January 2025](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/tech-and-ai/our-insights/superagency-in-the-workplace-empowering-people-to-unlock-ais-full-potential-at-work) that 88% of organizations say they use AI, but only 1% consider themselves "mature". The gap between "we're testing ChatGPT" and "we've actually transformed our workflows" is massive.

On productivity, studies contradict each other. Some say AI makes devs 19% slower. Others talk about +10% velocity. My observation: no universal "10x magic". But for those who master it, real, measurable gains. The key isn't just "using a tool". It's rethinking workflows.

## My last 3 months — the most satisfying video game

I wrote [A year after Hello World](https://tomandrieu.com/blog/un-an-apres-hello-world). Here's what happened these past 3 months. Before, learning a new stack took 2-3 weeks. Read the docs, test, break, start over. Lots of downtime. Now, learning a new stack takes 2-3 days. Claude guides me, explains, corrects in real-time. No downtime. I learn while producing.

The paradox is that before, either I learned or I produced. Now, I learn while producing. Claude explains while I code. I understand by building. And honestly, it's become an incredibly satisfying video game. Feedback loops are ultra-fast. I test an idea, Claude corrects me immediately. Iteration in seconds, not hours. My XP visibly rises fast. Every day I learn something new. Not just "how to code X", but "why architect like this", "how to think about this problem", "what's the right abstraction". Constant feeling of progression. New skills unlocked regularly. It's addictive.

What this teaches me goes beyond code. Architecture. System thinking. Product thinking. Claude doesn't just code for me, he explains why. I consider myself in the top 10% of devs with agentics. Not by innate talent. But because I've grinded intensively these past months. BMAD. Claude Code terminal-first. Spec-driven, test-driven. Olympus and Pantheon failed, but I learned. MVP BVN. scanr. Personal site. CBA hackathon this week. 1 week to recode a big chunk of the app. But this advantage won't last.

The gap will close in 18-24 months. Why? Because LLMs are becoming easier and easier to use. Anthropic, OpenAI, Google. All working to simplify UX. What took me 2 weeks to master will take 2 days in 1 year. Tools are improving exponentially. Claude Code, Cursor. All evolving fast. The learning curve is shrinking. Adoption is accelerating. 84% of devs use or plan to use AI. Every month, more people switch. My current advantage is temporary. Today's top 10% will be tomorrow's top 30%.

By end of 2027, tools will be trivial. Everyone will have access to the same power. The advantage will become pure talent again, not tool mastery. I need to take advantage of this gap now.

## Taking advantage of the gap to bring my vision

I have 18-24 months. Maybe less. To take advantage of this lead before it dilutes. Not just "code faster". That, anyone will do in 2 years. What matters is sharing the vision. Helping other teams make this transition.

Training and support. Current workflow audit. BMAD and Claude Code training. Pilot project support. Mandatory knowledge transfer. Not just deliver and leave. Not selling AI. Not "buy this magic SaaS". Transform the way of working. Structure human-AI collaboration. Not just for devs. Marketing, ops, support. Anywhere there are structurable workflows.

For who? Tech SMEs and startups. Development teams. Companies that feel the gap but don't know where to start. Why now? Because in 18-24 months, this expertise will be commoditized. Tools will be trivial. Everyone will have access. Today, I have an advantage. I can help others shorten their learning curve. Tomorrow, everyone will be at the same level.

## Keep learning, the train has left

This article isn't an ad. It's an observation. We're in a bubble. 10% of the world actually uses AI. The gap exists. It will close in 18-24 months. I'm on this train. Full speed. I'm learning, building, iterating. Every day. Every week. The most satisfying video game I've ever played. I'm going to take advantage of these 18-24 months. Grind the XP. Share what I learn. Help those who want to get on the train. After? The gap will close. Everyone will have access. And we'll move on to something else. But for now: keep learning. We'll see where it goes. The train has left.

A developer posts a video on X. Title: "I was a 10x engineer. Now I'm useless."

He talks about how his brain now refuses to code manually. How GPT 5.4 and Codex atrophied his ability to write code. He tried "vibe coding," deploying without review, blind trust in AI, and it worked. But he feels empty. Without identity.

The comments split into two camps. "Adapt or die." "These machines are making you stupid."

I watched this video at 00:30 in the morning. My first reaction: I relate.

It's real. My brain is fried. I certainly couldn't code a single line manually like I used to. The patterns I had in my head, the syntax shortcuts, the muscle memory when I typed TypeScript at full speed, all of it eroded. Gradually. Without me noticing.

But here's the difference: **I never mourned that loss.**

## The identity you lose vs the identity you find

The dev in the video lost his identity because he identified with code. Technique was who he was. Algorithmic elegance, clean architecture, language mastery, that defined his value. When LLMs took that capability, they took who he was.

Me? I was never a technician at heart.

I just want to help people. Code was the vehicle, not the destination. Angular, NestJS, clean architecture, those were means, not ends. I spent years learning these tools, and I regret nothing. But that wasn't my identity.

LLMs didn't take my identity. They **removed the obstacle** between me and what I actually wanted to do.

Result: I'm having more fun now. Because I see my users' relief faster. Because I can express what I've always wanted more quickly. I'm building [Bienvenue](https://tomandrieu.com), [Candineo](https://tomandrieu.com), [ScanR](https://tomandrieu.com), projects that help real people. Not because the architecture is elegant. Because somewhere, a user will be relieved.

## From "Building" to "Judgment"

That same evening, I come across a [tweet from @signulll](https://x.com/signulll/status/2030404483897815089) (868K views):

> "Building is no longer hard. The variance in outcomes has shifted almost entirely to judgment on what to build, how to sequence it, & how to talk about it."

And it clicks.

Before, the hierarchy in tech reflected the bottleneck: engineering. 10x engineers had the status because they could build what others couldn't. Now, everyone can build. The bottleneck has shifted.

What matters now: **what** to build, not how. **How** to sequence features. **How** to tell the product's story. Engineering is no longer the bottleneck. Judgment is.

[The article I wrote yesterday](https://tomandrieu.com/blog/gap-adoption-ia) discussed the productivity plateau at 10%. DX Research measured 121,000 developers. 92.6% use AI, but gains have stagnated around 10% since mid-2025. That number measures the wrong thing. It measures the speed at which we produce code. But code is no longer the bottleneck. The real shift isn't in individual productivity. It's in **who creates value**.

## The Product Thinker

@signulll proposes a new name: "product thinker." Not product manager. Not tech lead. Something in between.

> "The rarest version of this person sits at the intersection of culture & deep technology. Someone genuinely bilingual. They know what's technically possible & they know which cultural currents are real vs. ephemeral."

Someone who understands what's technically possible, who understands what resonates culturally, who holds in their head where the product should be in 2 years, and who tells the story that organizes the team and shapes the user experience.

Before, this person was rare but not critical. Engineering was the bottleneck. Now? "They might be the most important person in the room."

In January, in [Hello World](https://tomandrieu.com/blog/hello-world), I talked about the Product Engineer. PostHog's concept. A developer responsible for the entire product experience, from code to user impact. Two months later, the concept has evolved. The Product Engineer was still rooted in engineering. The Product Thinker is broader. Someone whose primary value isn't in the code they produce, but in the decisions they make about what to build and why.

## What this changes for me

I'm not a 10x engineer. I never was.

But I know what helps people. I know what relieves them. I know Angular and NestJS enough to understand what's feasible. I know enough stoicism and buddhism to know what resonates beyond the technical.

My workflow today: I define specs, I sequence priorities, I steer [Claude Code](https://docs.anthropic.com/en/docs/agents-and-tools/claude-code/overview) for implementation. The "building" is delegated. What remains is judgment. Sequencing. The story. That's exactly what I wanted to do from the start.

In [AI amplifies what already existed](https://tomandrieu.com/blog/ia-amplifie), I wrote about how two weeks with AI agents confirmed that my vision (specs, docs, rigor) was heading in the right direction. AI doesn't compensate for a fuzzy process. It exposes it. Brutally. And it's the same with identity. AI doesn't create an identity crisis. It exposes an identity that wasn't solid.

## The real divide

The industry is splitting. Not between those who use AI and those who resist. Between those whose identity was in code and those whose identity was elsewhere.

The first group mourns what they lose. The second uses what they gain.

10x engineers aren't becoming obsolete because AI codes better. They're becoming obsolete because coding is no longer the bottleneck. Product thinkers are becoming rare. Because everyone can code now, but few people know **what** to code and **why**.

People ask: "Will AI replace developers?" Wrong question. The real question: **who were you really, underneath the code?**

If you were a pure technician, LLMs take your identity. You become the dev from the video. If you were a helper, a builder, a product thinker, LLMs just remove the obstacle. The difference isn't in the tool. It's in the identity you had before the tool arrived.

My brain is fried for manual coding? Yes. But I've never had this much clarity on what I actually want to do.

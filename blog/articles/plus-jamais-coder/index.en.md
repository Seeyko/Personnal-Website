It's been several weeks, maybe months, since I've written almost any line of code. On my personal projects, at work, everywhere. But tonight I really verbalized it for the first time. I don't think it'll happen again.

During the [hackathon](https://tomandrieu.com/blog/hackathon-ia), I designed, coordinated, validated, iterated with agents. Code came out. Not always right on the first try, but that's not what we iterate on anymore. It's the direction, the specs, understanding the need. The code itself has become a byproduct of the thinking. And looking back, it had been like that for a while already. I start projects, test things, iterate, fail and try to be more informed the second time around. I learn from my mistakes and from my peers, by talking a lot. But I don't code anymore.

Boris Cherny, the creator of Claude Code at Anthropic, [hasn't written a single line of code by hand since November](https://x.com/lennysan/status/2024896611818897438). 100% AI-generated, 20+ PRs a day, and he's still one of the most productive engineers at the company. He thinks the entry-level "software engineer" role could effectively disappear by end of 2026. And that the next job to be impacted is "any kind of job where you use computer tools." So pretty much everyone.

Me, front-end lead dev, 7 years of experience, I built part of my identity on being a good coder. And I don't code anymore. It's weird to put it in writing. Not sad, not scary, just weird. Like realizing you no longer do something you thought defined who you are.

## What's happening around

Meta planning to cut 20% of its workforce to fund AI investments. Dell cutting 11,000 jobs in a year. Jack Dorsey cutting Block from 10,000 to under 6,000 people and [telling investors](https://x.com/aakashgupta/status/2034130787847573660) that "most companies are late" to do the same. Meta's stock climbed 3% the day they announced the layoffs. Markets applaud when you fire people to replace them with AI.

And at the same time, the majority of companies that invested in generative AI have seen almost zero return on investment. Tens of billions invested, not much to show for it. Even Silicon Valley is starting to admit we might be in a bubble.

Either the bubble pops and it's economic disappointment. Or AI delivers on its promises and it's the societal transformation nobody's ready for. Either way, it's going to shake things up. OpenAI and Anthropic fighting over Pentagon contracts. Sam Altman who was talking about universal basic income a year ago and has been oddly silent on the topic for months. The hegemony of a provider that achieves AGI first, tied to this insane capitalist race, is going to hurt when we hit the wall. Whether through disappointment or success.

The most exposed jobs are educated white-collar workers. The people in the hackathon room. Me. Will AI create as many jobs as it destroys? Are we headed toward universal basic income, collapse, or something better? Nobody knows.

## And me

It's a strength to have met people who understand me and want to move forward together, and with whom I think we'll do real things. Does all of this point me toward a future I don't control at all? Probably. But there's no point worrying about things beyond our scale. What I control is what I learn today, the people I work with, and the direction I take. If the wall comes, I'll pivot. All I can do is try to prepare and talk about it with people around me.

[I estimated I had an 18-24 month head start.](https://tomandrieu.com/blog/gap-adoption-ia) I can feel it shrinking as models advance and people start using them. But in the meantime, I iterate, I fail, I move forward.
